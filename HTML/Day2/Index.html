The Stone Cutter's Dream

Once upon a time, there was a young stone cutter named Arun who lived in a small village. Every day, he worked under the hot sun, cutting large stones with a hammer and chisel. His work was difficult, and his hands were always covered with dust and small cuts. Whenever he looked at the rich people in the village, he felt unhappy. He thought, "Why am I working so hard while others live such comfortable lives? I wish I could become rich." One day, an old traveler came to the village. Seeing Arun's sad face, the traveler asked, "Why do you look so unhappy?" Arun replied, "I work every day, but I still have very little. I wish my life were easier." The traveler smiled and said, "Success does not come from wishing. It comes from learning, improving, and never giving up." Arun did not understand those words at first, but he kept thinking about them. The next day, instead of complaining, he decided to improve his skills. He learned better ways to cut stones. He observed experienced workers, asked questions, and practiced every evening after work. Whenever he made a mistake, he treated it as a lesson instead of a failure. Months passed. People began to notice the quality of Arun's work. Soon, he received bigger projects. His earnings increased, and he hired other workers to help him. Years later, Arun owned one of the largest stone workshops in the region. Many young people came to him to learn the craft. One day, the same old traveler returned to the village. He saw Arun smiling while teaching others. The traveler asked, "Do you remember what I told you years ago?" Arun smiled and replied, "Yes. I wanted success to come quickly, but I learned that success is built one small step at a time. Every mistake taught me something new, and every day of practice made me stronger." The traveler nodded with pride. Arun then told his students, "Never compare your beginning with someone else's success. Everyone starts as a beginner. The difference between successful people and unsuccessful people is not talent—it is consistency. If you keep learning and never stop trying, one day your hard work will become your success." From that day onward, Arun continued to teach not only stone cutting but also the importance of patience, discipline, and continuous learning.
